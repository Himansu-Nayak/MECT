## 1. Strategic direction

MAHAVIR TRUST must be presented as a credible, professionally governed, multi-sector charitable development organisation — not as an education-only organisation.

Education remains a major and important area of work, but the public identity must communicate the trust's wider commitment to people, families and communities. The website should explain the relationship between learning, livelihoods, women's agency, safe water, nutrition and community welfare.

**Proposed positioning statement**

> A multi-sector charitable development organisation working to strengthen people, families and communities through education, women's empowerment, livelihoods, safe water, nutrition and community welfare.

This is a proposed communications position, not a claim that MECT operates every development service or sector represented by larger national NGOs.

**Proposed brand promise**

> Creating opportunities. Strengthening communities. Enabling lasting change.

The trust's own approved mission, deed and programme records remain the source of truth. This line must be reviewed by the trust before publication as a formal tagline.

## 2. Reference strategy: what to learn from Smile Foundation

Use Smile Foundation as a benchmark for organisational completeness and content architecture, not as copy to reproduce.

Learn from its:
- Organisation-first positioning rather than one-service positioning.
- Clear separation of About, Our Work, Campaigns, Get Involved, Media Centre and Resource Centre.
- Dedicated sector/programme narratives with a problem statement, response, interventions, outcomes and stories.
- Impact reporting that connects numbers to real people, geographies and programme outcomes.
- Distinct cause/campaign pages that help visitors understand and support a specific initiative.
- Clear participation paths for individuals, volunteers, institutions and corporate/CSR partners.
- Governance, leadership, reach, annual reports, stories, films, news and knowledge resources.
- Consistent journeys from a programme story to impact evidence and a relevant action.

Do not copy Smile Foundation's wording, campaign names, logos, images, brand palette, visual assets, statistics or proprietary design. Create MECT's own narrative and design language around its verified work and Odisha context.

## 3. MECT's current evidence-backed development areas

The following matrix is derived from the current repository content and must be checked against original trust records before public launch.

| Public-facing development area | Existing MECT work that supports it | Content boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Education & learning | Shiksha Setu remedial education, scholarships/tuition support, study materials, academic mentorship and career orientation | Do not imply a full school network or broad statewide operations unless evidenced |
| Women empowerment & livelihoods | Swavalamban tailoring, embroidery and vocational skill development | Describe actual training and livelihood support; do not claim placement rates or enterprise numbers without records |
| Safe water, sanitation & community wellbeing | Jal Seva drinking-water access, summer hydration points and sanitation activities | Do not label this as a hospital, clinical healthcare or mobile medical service |
| Food security & nutrition | Jan Ahar meals/food relief for patients' attendants, destitute families and other documented recipients | Verify programme period, locations, beneficiary totals and current activity before publishing metrics |
| Community welfare & grassroots outreach | Local community support and documented emergency-relief activity | Avoid implying a national disaster-response network or permanent relief force |

**Important distinction:** Water, sanitation and nutrition contribute to public wellbeing, but they are not evidence that MECT provides clinical healthcare. Healthcare camps, clinics, telemedicine, child protection, environment programmes, disaster-response operations and other new sectors must not be presented as live MECT programmes until there is documentation, an approved plan and delivery evidence.

### Active, partner-led and future work

Every programme and sector page must label the delivery status honestly:
- **Active MECT programme:** operational activity supported by current records.
- **Partner-led / collaborative initiative:** delivered with a named, verified partner and clear responsibilities.
- **Planned / future area:** strategic ambition only; never styled or worded as an existing service.
- **Archived initiative:** historical work, with the period clearly stated.

Do not convert a CSR-eligible area or a broad statutory objects clause into a claim of operational delivery.

## 4. Organisational narrative

The site should tell a coherent story:

1. **Who we are:** a registered charitable trust rooted in Odisha.
2. **Why we exist:** people face interconnected barriers; education, economic opportunity and essential community support reinforce one another.
3. **Where we work:** only verified locations, districts and project geographies.
4. **What we do:** evidence-backed programmes, each with a defined need, activity and outcome.
5. **How we work:** community participation, local relationships, responsible partnerships, fiduciary care and transparent use of resources.
6. **What changes:** measured outputs and outcomes, human stories with consent, and clear limitations.
7. **How to participate:** individual support, volunteering, institutional collaboration and CSR partnerships.
8. **How to verify us:** governance, statutory disclosures, reports, financial records and contact information.

The central idea should be a connected community-development approach — not a claim that every social sector is already covered by MECT.

## 6. Homepage content architecture

The redesigned homepage should be a clear institutional story with strong visual rhythm and multiple ways to engage.

1. **Header:** restrained logo/navigation; clear Our Work, Impact, About, Get Involved and Contact routes; one primary donation/support action.
2. **Hero:** strong multi-sector positioning, authentic community image(s), concise explanation and two clear actions. Do not make the hero education-only.
3. **Proof / trust strip:** registration and accreditation references linked to the statutory vault. Avoid an oversized continuously moving ticker that competes with reading.
4. **About MECT:** a short organisation-first introduction and a link to the full story.
5. **Our areas of work:** visually distinct development-area cards mapped to the five evidence-backed areas above.
6. **Programmes in action:** a deeper look at named MECT initiatives (Shiksha Setu, Swavalamban, Jal Seva and Jan Ahar) with status, place, activity and a relevant route.
7. **How change happens:** a simple connected model showing people, families, local institutions and communities; avoid copying Smile Foundation's lifecycle labels.
8. **Impact:** carefully sourced metrics with measurement period, geography and definition. Distinguish reach, outputs and outcomes.
9. **Stories from the field:** real, consented stories and photos; never invent beneficiaries, quotes or case studies.
10. **Work with us:** individual giving, volunteering, institutional and CSR partnerships.
11. **Transparency:** annual reports, governance, statutory disclosures and direct-disbursal policy, with claims phrased precisely.
12. **Latest updates:** add only when the organisation can maintain an editorial feed.
13. **Footer:** concise navigation, office/contact details, trust identity, reports and legal links.

The existing scholarship eligibility tool, scheme discovery, application, tracking, volunteer, inquiry, donation and HIm AI experiences must be preserved. Place these tools where they serve a visitor's task instead of allowing them to overwhelm the organisation story.

## 7. Content rules and editorial standards

- Write in clear, dignified, people-first language. Avoid self-congratulatory NGO jargon and exaggerated claims.
- Every programme page should answer: What challenge? Who is served? Where? What does MECT do? Who delivers it? What evidence exists? How can someone participate?
- Attach a period and definition to every metric (for example, cumulative vs annual, direct vs indirect reach).
- Do not reuse an old number as a current number without revalidation.
- Do not turn targets, proposals or CSR eligibility into achievements.
- Use beneficiary names, images and testimonials only with informed consent and appropriate safeguarding.
- Never create fictional stories or AI-generated documentary imagery that appears to depict real MECT beneficiaries.
- Clearly identify partner-delivered work and the partner's role.
- Separate public programme content from sensitive student, donor, volunteer and administrative information.
- Ensure English and Odia content is reviewed for meaning and readability before publication.

## 8. Visual identity and design constraints

### Preserve the current brand family
- Forest Green: `#0B3B24`
- Deep Forest: `#072617`
- Forest Light: `#165B38`
- Heritage Gold: `#C5A059`
- Champagne Gold: `#E4C88A`
- Warm Ivory: `#FDFBF7`
- Warm Surface: `#F4EFE6`
- Sand: `#EDE6D8`
- Charcoal: `#1C2421`
- Slate Text: `#4A5568`

**Do not introduce or extend institutional navy (#0A192F), navy-deep (#0F172A), or royal-blue-led surfaces into redesigned components.** Where the older UI uses navy, phase it out of redesigned areas in favour of the established forest-green and warm-neutral palette. Do not create a new unrelated palette.

### Visual direction
- Human-centred, editorial, confident, warm and institutionally credible.
- Use authentic, high-quality photographs from MECT's own programmes where consent exists.
- Use clear image composition, asymmetric editorial layouts, generous whitespace, strong typography, restrained card surfaces and purposeful motion.
- Borrow the storytelling rhythm of the Farzan Faruk / Rylic Studio mental-health reference and the education-oriented layouts, but adapt them to MECT's own content and palette.
- Never reproduce another organisation's exact UI, artwork or photography.
- Avoid generic AI-looking illustrations as substitutes for actual field evidence.
- Design mobile-first; support 360px through wide desktop layouts; respect reduced motion and keyboard access.

## 9. Technical implementation guardrails

- Preserve existing Next.js route contracts and the current App Router structure.
- Preserve backend API contracts, database schema/migrations, authentication, admin operations and AI gateway behaviour.
- Reuse existing components where possible; refactor oversized client components carefully.
- Keep content in typed, centralised data structures rather than hardcoding the same text in multiple pages.
- Prefer server components for static narrative sections; use client components only for actual interaction.
- Do not change donation, scholarship, volunteer or inquiry workflows as part of a visual-only change.
- Use local/approved image assets and explicit image rights/consent; do not hotlink unverified third-party images.
- Every visual integration must be checked for accessibility, mobile overflow, route validity, broken links, TypeScript, lint and production build.
- Read the installed Next.js 16 documentation before modifying Next.js APIs, as required by `AGENTS.md`.
